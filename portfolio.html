01

An awareness problem that wasn't really about awareness.

Graduate students at UT Austin knew the Office of Career and Life Design existed. They didn't engage.

Client research surfaced three patterns: engagement clusters around qualifying exams and defenses (the office worked as a fallback, not a draw), emails reached inboxes but didn't convert, and graduate identity sits outside the "career advising" frame.

A 12-institution competitive analysis confirmed the diagnosis. OCLD's Life Design framework is uncontested in the higher-ed career landscape, but its student-facing language doesn't surface the distinction at first contact. Students arrive with competitor-trained expectations of a transactional service and read OCLD that way by default.

The brief was simple. Build a creative activation that interrupts that miscategorization and routes the right student to the right kind of first appointment, with no additional staffing burden.

02

Four functions. Two layers.

Functional Attitude Theory (Katz, 1960) treats an attitude as four independent psychological functions. Each is a different barrier and needs a different message — generic outreach fails everyone at once.

But matched messaging only works if the audience has correctly categorized the attitude object. OCLD's positioning fails this precondition. Any matched message lands on the wrong frame. The campaign therefore operates in two layers: recategorize first (Life Design is a self-awareness framework, not a career service), then match (deliver function-specific persuasion).

Value-Expressive

Penelope

Loyal to who she is, no matter what suitor arrives at the door. Asks where her depth has the most leverage.

Knowledge

Tiresias

The blind prophet who maps what others can't see yet. Won't move until the terrain is visible.

Ego-Defensive

Calypso

Her island is safe because nobody can find her. The hesitation isn't avoidance — it's protection.

Utilitarian

Odysseus

Ties himself to the mast before the song begins. Designs the structure that survives his own desires.

04

Six UX decisions that did most of the work.

D · 01

Paintings lead, text follows.

Graduate students are scanning, not reading. Each scene opens with a 16:9 cinematic plate from a classical Odyssey painting. The setup is one or two short sentences underneath. The painting carries the emotional context the words would otherwise have to spell out.

Cognitive offload
D · 02

Names withheld until the reveal.

An earlier version showed all four characters upfront. Cut. Forcing students to learn four mythological names before answering anything broke flow and gave away the diagnostic. Now the names appear only on the result screen — the reveal becomes the payoff.

Engagement
D · 03

One scene, one color.

Each scene runs on its own accent: sea teal, umber, wine, blood, gold, olive. The accent threads through HP bar fill, selected option border, and pulse glow on the continue button. By Scene 4 the student feels they're in a different room, not on the same page with new text.

Atmosphere
D · 04

Page-by-page, not scrolling doc.

Each scene fits roughly one viewport. Slide-in transition between scenes. Touch swipe enabled on mobile. The student moves the way a reader turns a page — small commitments, low overhead, no sense of "how much more is left."

Flow
D · 05

Tie scoring made visible.

First version silently declared a winner when answers tied. Felt arbitrary. Now ties surface a small note: "Your answers split across two patterns. The last scene weighed most, because that one tests how you actually decide in the moment." The math becomes part of the explanation, not a black box.

Trust
D · 06

Result CTAs are matched, not generic.

The four result pages don't link to the same booking page with different copy. They route to four different first-appointment types: Career Design Conversation (Penelope), Overview Session (Tiresias), Confidential 1:1 (Calypso), Strategy Session (Odysseus). The function-matched message reaches the function-matched service.

Conversion

06

Built to scale to zero staff.

OCLD has three full-time staff for graduate programs. Any creative activation that requires ongoing maintenance fails before it ships. The quiz was built as a single self-contained HTML file, no framework, no build step, no backend.

It deploys to GitHub Pages or any static host in under a minute, embeds inside Canvas as an iframe, opens from a QR code on a faculty-distributed folder, and passes UTM source parameters back to OCLD's existing Handshake intake so each entry channel can be measured independently.

Once the shell is built, the marginal cost per student is zero. The campaign scales to every incoming master's and PhD cohort across 14 colleges with no additional headcount.
